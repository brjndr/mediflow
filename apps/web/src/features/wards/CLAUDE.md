# Wards, rooms and beds: module notes

What this module is and what was decided for it. The root `CLAUDE.md` holds the rules every module follows, and it wins where the two disagree. Keep this file current with the module.

## What exists

**Wards, rooms and beds (web and API):** the wards of the hospital (code, name, floor or block, department), the rooms of each and the beds of each room, with adding, changing, archiving and restoring at every level; and bed categories (general ward, private room, intensive care), each charged as a bed-day service of the price catalog (#65). Behind the `wards` switch and the `ward:read` and `ward:manage` permissions. Desktop-only. Decided for now: every bed is in a room, and an open ward is one room with many beds; the tariff is on the bed category as a link to a bed-day service, never a price typed on a bed, and only the service's name is shown here; a bed is `available`, `occupied`, `cleaning` or `blocked`, and from this screen it is only blocked, with a reason from a list and an optional note about the bed (free text is where something about a patient gets written, so the reason is not free text), and put back in use: occupied and being cleaned are set by admissions and housekeeping (R2) and the server checks every change of status on the bed as it is; a ward or a room with an occupied bed, and an occupied bed, cannot be archived; a room is archived with its beds and comes back without them; archiving never changes a bed's status, so a bed being cleaned or blocked is still that when it is restored; an admission keeps the category and service of its bed as they were when the patient was given it, so a change here applies to stays that start after it; nothing of an archived ward is changed; a bed number is one in its ward and a room name too. The category's service is chosen through `useServiceOptions` from `@/features/services`, and the ward's department through `useDepartmentOptions`.

The API (#301) is described in `apps/api/CLAUDE.md`, under Wards, rooms and beds. Where it differs from the mock: the list of wards is in the order the database gives their lower-cased names, so "Ward 10" comes before "Ward 2" there (rooms and beds inside a ward are in the order people count them, as in the mock); a `code` sent with a change of a ward, and a `status` sent with a bed, is dropped, not refused; text is stored tidied, and a control character in it is a 400 (#306); a ward has at most 200 rooms and 1000 beds, archived ones counted (409 `ward_room_limit`, `ward_bed_limit`, for which this screen has no message of its own yet); a bad body is refused before the ward is looked for; and whoever changes a ward needs `ward:read` as well as `ward:manage`, because the answer is the ward. A repeat of adding a room or a bed with the same key is answered with the ward as it is then, as the mock does.
