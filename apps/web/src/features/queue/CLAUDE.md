# OPD queue: module notes

What this module is and what was decided for it. The root `CLAUDE.md` holds the rules every module follows, and it wins where the two disagree. Keep this file current with the module.

## What exists

**The queue (web and API; the API is #79, notes in `apps/api/src/features/queue/CLAUDE.md`):** today's OPD tokens at the hospital, per department and doctor. Behind the `queue` switch and the `queue:read`, `queue:create` and `queue:call` permissions. Works at every width.

**Issuing a token (#76):** `queue/new`, behind `queue:create`. The patient is found with `PatientSearch` from `@/features/patients`, then a department (`useDepartmentOptions`), optionally a doctor of that department (`useDoctorOptions`, narrowed by its `departmentId`) and optionally a priority. The screen confirms the token's number in place, with "Issue another" keeping the department and the doctor. "Issue a queue token" on the patient's screen (the `patient.detail.actions` slot, for `queue:create`) opens it at `queue/new?patientId=<id>`, with the patient already chosen through `usePatientName`, so a walk-in registered a moment ago is not searched for again.

**The board (#76):** `queue`, behind `queue:read`. Three groups, each a `DataGrid` of its own page from the server: now serving (`called`), waiting and skipped, in call order. Filtered by department and by doctor, and it opens on the signed-in doctor's own queue, as the appointment list does. For `queue:call`: "Call next for <doctor>" when a doctor is chosen, "Call again" and "Skip" on a token being served, "Back in the queue" on a skipped one, and "Change priority" (a panel beside the groups) on a waiting or skipped one.

**Checking in (#376):** "Check in" on today's scheduled appointment, in a row of the appointment list (`appointment.row.actions`, under the patient's name) and on the appointment's own screen (`appointment.detail.actions`), for `queue:create` and `patient:read`. One action, no search: `POST /queue/check-ins` with the appointment's id issues a token for its doctor, in the doctor's department, and the action gives way to "Checked in: token 7, General Medicine." in a status region, which takes focus.

## Decided

- **One queue per department per day, at the hospital's own day.** The server decides what today is (the hospital's timezone) and gives the numbers: one sequence per department per day, 1, 2, 3, safe when two desks issue at once. The browser never assigns a number or asks for another day. Two departments both have a token 3: a token is named with its patient on the screen.
- **A token is for a department and may name a doctor of it.** A doctor's queue is the tokens issued for them and the tokens of their department issued for no doctor. "Call next" takes the first of those and gives it to the doctor. A doctor not in the department is 422 `doctor_not_in_department`.
- **Call order: a priority first, then the place in the line** (the number breaks a tie). A walk-in's place is the time issued. **A booked patient who checks in before their booked time is placed at the booked time; one who comes after it, at the time they checked in (#376):** a booking is honoured without letting an early arrival go ahead of walk-ins who came before that time, and a late one loses the booked place. The server sets the place once (`line_at`, `greatest(now(), starts_at)`); a priority still goes first. The tokens being served are in the order they were called. The order is the server's: the screen shows what it gives.
- **A fixed list of priorities:** senior citizen, pregnancy, disability, child, clinical urgency. Shown as a word with an icon. A hospital's own list needs a setting, an additive change later.
- **Statuses: `waiting -> called -> served`, `called -> skipped`, `skipped -> waiting`.** "Call next" makes the token the doctor was serving `served`: there is no separate "Done" button, as a doctor calls the next patient when the last one leaves. "Call again" repeats the call of the token being served (`callCount` goes up). "Back in the queue" puts a skipped patient back at the place their priority and place in the line give them, not at the end. Served tokens are not on the board. The API enforces these (409 `invalid_transition`); `allowedActions` in `schemas.ts` offers the same on the screen.
- **Nobody waiting is 409 `queue_empty`,** said under the filters. A patient with a token not yet served in that department today is refused a second one (409 `already_queued`).
- **Every change carries an idempotency key** (one per request body, kept until one succeeds, so a retry after a failure is answered with the first result; one `useQueueAction` serves every row, so a second row's action starts a key of its own) and, except "Call next", the token's `version`. Someone else's change first gives 409 `conflict`: the screen says so and reads the queue again. Nothing is shown before the server answers: whom a doctor calls is the server's decision, and another desk may have called the same patient.
- **Permissions:** `queue:read` (the board), `queue:create` (issuing; the desk), `queue:call` (calling, skipping, recalling, priority; doctors and nurses). Default grants for the API's seed (#79) follow the "Appointments and queue" and "Triage and vitals" rows of `docs/PRODUCT.md`: admin all three, receptionist read and create, doctor and nurse read and call. A nurse calls for a doctor by choosing the doctor. The mock has no nurse role; its admin, doctor and receptionist have these grants.
- **Issuing needs `patient:read` as well as `queue:create`, on the server too.** The answer names the patient and their MRN, so a role that may not read patients is refused, with the same 422 `unknown_patient` as an id that does not exist: it learns nothing about one. The screen says what is missing before anything is sent.
- **The board follows server-sent events (#76).** `GET /queue/events` is a stream for the session's hospital only; a `queue` event says which department changed (`{"departmentId":"..."}`), never who, and the screen then reads its groups again through `GET /queue/tokens`, which the API audits. Events within 300 ms are one refresh, and a steady flow still refreshes at least once a second. A board shown for one department, or for one doctor (whose queue is all in their department), refreshes only for that department's events; the filter changes without reopening the stream. The stream is read with `followEventStream` from `shared/api`, through the API client (cookie, tenant header, error handling), and closed on leaving the screen, on a switch of hospital (a new stream opens for the new one) and on sign-out. Each time it opens, the first time included, the queue is read once, for what changed before it was open (the lists were read first). The server sends a keep-alive comment every 20 seconds; a stream that brings nothing for 45 seconds is taken as lost, since a dropped connection does not always close. It opens again after a break with a growing wait with jitter, the server's `retry:` (kept between 1 and 30 seconds) or its Retry-After; a stream the server closes within 10 seconds of opening counts as a failure, so a proxy that drops every stream is not reopened again and again. A 401, 403, 404 or 409 stops it for good. The server (#79) ends the stream when the session ends, is revoked or switches hospital, or loses `queue:read`, and checks the session before each event: the screen closing its own stream is not enough. While it is not open, the board reads the queue every 15 seconds (`FALLBACK_REFRESH_MS`), and says so.
- **Only what this person did is announced.** The status line, which is always on the page, says "Token 4, <patient>, called." and the like. A change by someone else is not announced: on a busy board it would never stop. The counts of the groups are plain text for the same reason. The live line under the heading says, in words with an icon, whether changes arrive within seconds.
- **Focus is never left on the page.** A row that moves to another group takes focus with it, so focus goes to the status line. The priority panel's heading takes focus when it opens, and focus goes back to its "Change priority" button when it closes (`usePanelFocus`). The panel is part of the page, not a dialog: it closes with its own button.
- **The priority reason is health information** (pregnancy, disability, clinical urgency) shown beside the name on this staff board only. The waiting-room display (#78) shows the token number and where to go, never the name, the MRN or the reason, and does not reuse this response shape.
- **The board names the patient to anyone who may read the queue,** by name and masked MRN, without `patient:read`, as the appointment list does: a token means nothing without whom it is for.
- **The board hosts the `queue.row.actions` slot (#77),** under each token's patient, given `tokenId`, `patientId`, `number` and `status` only. A contribution is rendered for every row, so it reads no data of its own: triage adds "Record vitals" there.
- **Check-in is one token per appointment, ever (#376).** Checking it in again, from any desk, answers with the first token (200 instead of 201) and the action says "Already checked in: token 7, …", so a second desk sees the number rather than an error. Only a scheduled appointment that starts today at the hospital is offered it (the screen checks the day in the hospital's timezone, the server again: 409 `invalid_transition`, 422 `not_today`). A patient already waiting in that department as a walk-in is 409 `already_queued`, said beside the action. The contribution is given `appointmentId`, `status` and `startsAt` and reads no data; its button is named by the appointment's time ("Check in, appointment at 11:30 am"), not the patient, which the row already shows. It needs `patient:read` because the answer names the patient: the manifest can require only the queue's own permissions, so the component checks it. The appointment keeps its status: completing it is the encounter's (M7).
- **The token form is opened from a patient with an id only** (`?patientId`); a malformed one is ignored. The name comes from the patients feature under its own permission (`usePatientName`, which hands over the name and MRN only), never from the address or the history state; when it cannot be read the screen says so and offers the search.

## Mock

`mocks/handlers/queue.ts` seeds today's queue at each hospital: at the hospital, five tokens in the first department (one being served by `doc_h_01`, one skipped, one with a priority, two for any doctor), two in the second and one with a priority in the third; at the clinic, two in its first department. It keeps ids only and looks names up when it answers, checks in the appointments mock's appointments (`mockAppointment`) with the API's rules and order, sends a keep-alive comment every 20 seconds on each stream, and refuses issuing without `patient:read` as the API will. A change is decided with nothing awaited between the check and the change, and is published to the hospital's open streams. Tests change the queue behind the screen with `addMockQueueToken`, close the streams with `dropMockQueueStreams`, and count the streams opened with `mockQueueStreams`. MSW does not pass on a stream the browser closed, so the mock never hears one end: tests check that the screen stops acting on events, not that the mock saw it go.

## Known gaps

- A checked-in appointment's row does not say so until the action is used: a row reads no data of its own, and the list has no token in its answer.
- A token issued by mistake cannot be cancelled.
- A token does not open the OPD encounter: the doctor opens one from it when the consultation starts (`POST /encounters` with `queueTokenId`, #86), not on `queue.called`, as a called patient may be skipped.
- The doctor filter offers up to 100 practising doctors, as `useDoctorOptions` does.
- A board left open past midnight at the hospital shows the new day's queue on its next read: the server decides the day.
