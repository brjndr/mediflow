# Clinical API

What was decided for this feature's API (#86, BE-12). Claude Code loads it when a file of this folder is read. The web side of encounters, notes and diagnoses is #80 (`apps/web/src/features/clinical/CLAUDE.md`); the other screens are #81 to #85, #87 to #89, and they build against this contract.

- **`features/clinical`** is the encounter, its notes and diagnoses, and the one table of orders with the prescriptions that place medication orders. Behind the `clinical` module flag. Built API first, with no planned contract: the web issues take these names and codes.
- **Permissions and defaults** (the "Clinical notes, diagnosis, orders" row of `docs/PRODUCT.md`): `encounter:read`, `order:read`, `order:update` and `prescription:read` go to doctor, nurse and admin; `encounter:create`, `encounter:update`, `clinicalNote:read`, `clinicalNote:write`, `diagnosis:read`, `diagnosis:write`, `order:create`, `order:cancel` and `prescription:create` go to doctor and admin. That is the nurse's limited view: the encounter, its orders and prescriptions, and moving an order on, with no notes and no diagnoses. **The admin holds every one, beyond the "view" of the matrix,** as with triage: nobody invites into a role that holds more than their own (`grantsWithin`), so an admin without them could not invite a doctor or a nurse. A hospital narrows its admin once role management exists (BE-16).
- **Diagnoses are their own permission** (`diagnosis:read`), the field-level restriction the issue asked for: a role sees a visit without what it was for. They are their own table and routes, never a field of the encounter, so no response has to be masked. Diagnosis text written inside a note is behind `clinicalNote:read`.
- **Scope is the encounter's.** `all`; `own`, the encounters the caller attends as a doctor; `department`, those of the departments of the caller's active doctor profile; someone with no profile sees nothing under either. A note, diagnosis, order or prescription is found by id, then its encounter within the scope of the route's own permission; outside it is the same 404 (`encounter_not_found`, `note_not_found`, `diagnosis_not_found`, `order_not_found`, `prescription_not_found`) as another hospital's. Lists leave out what is outside the scope, never refuse. A row is locked only once its scope is checked, so a caller outside it cannot hold another doctor's record locked.
- **Opening** (`POST /encounters`) is explicit, when the doctor starts the consultation, not on `queue.called` (the queue's notes had suggested that): a patient called and then skipped would leave an empty encounter. Only `opd` is opened; the admission and ER modules add `ipd` and `er`. From a token of today of the patient (`tokenForEncounter` from `features/queue`), an appointment of the patient within the caller's `appointment:read` and with the appointments module on (`appointmentForCheckIn` from `features/appointments`; not a cancelled or no-show one), or a walk-in. The doctor is the one named, else the token's, else the appointment's, else the caller's own profile, practising and within the scope of `encounter:create`; the department is the doctor's. Opening needs `patient:read` too, in the handler, as the queue's issuing does.
- **A token checked in from an appointment brings the appointment** (`tokenForEncounter` gives it), so opening from the token and later from the appointment finds one visit, and `encounter.closed` names the appointment; a different appointment sent with the token is 422 `unknown_appointment`. A token is taken only while the queue module is on.
- **One encounter per token and per appointment** that is not cancelled: partial unique indexes. Asking again answers 200 with that encounter; two at the same moment are a 201 and a 409 `already_open`, and so is a token and an appointment that each opened their own, or one the caller does not reach (never handed out). A cancelled one frees its token or appointment.
- **Closing** refuses while any note is a draft, whoever wrote it (409 `unsigned_notes`). **Cancelling** is for an encounter opened by mistake and only while nothing is on its record (409 `has_records`). Cancelling also refuses a draft (409 `unsigned_notes`), which a cancelled encounter could never sign. Both final, in the guard trigger too, which also keeps the patient, type, links and opening. Nothing new is added to an encounter that is not open (409 `encounter_not_open`, checked on the row locked `for share`, so it cannot close meanwhile); amending a note, retracting a diagnosis and moving an order on still work after it closes.
- **Notes are drafts, then signed, then never changed.** A draft is kept on the server and only its author reads, edits (`PATCH` with the version, 409 `conflict`), signs or discards it; the list gives the signed notes and the caller's own drafts. A signed note is refused any UPDATE or DELETE by a trigger. **An amendment is a new signed note** with the whole corrected text, `amends_id` and a reason, written by anyone with `clinicalNote:write`; a note is amended once (`clinical_notes_amended_once`, 409 `already_amended`), so a later correction amends the amendment and the history is one line. The amended note names its amendment (`amendedById`). The body is the app's rich text document, reduced to its allowlist on the way in and out, and an empty one is refused.
- **Diagnoses** are an ICD-10 code (`diagnosisCode` from `features/codes`), text, or both, provisional or final, with the code and title stored as they read when recorded (a set loaded again may reword them). Never changed: retracted once, with who, when and why, and kept. A confirmed provisional diagnosis is recorded again as final.
- **Orders:** one table, a type and exactly the catalog reference it takes (`lab_test_id`, `service_id` for imaging and procedure, `drug_id` for medication, none for diet), checked by the table. The item's code and name are stored as they read when ordered (`orderableLabTest`, `orderableService` with the category, `prescribableDrug`, each exported by its catalog module). One order per request, so the idempotency key names one record. The state machine (`placed -> accepted -> in_progress -> completed`, cancel with a reason from placed or accepted) is in the handler and in the guard trigger, which also keeps what was ordered. Medication is never placed on its own: it is a line of a prescription.
- **Prescriptions are never changed or removed** (the app role may only insert and read). A revision is a new prescription with `replaces_id` and a reason, once (`prescriptions_replaced_once`, 409 `already_replaced`; a state that refuses the change is a 409 throughout the module); it cancels the lines of the old one that have not started, with the replacement's reason, and leaves those in progress or completed. Writing a revision needs `prescription:create` only, not `order:cancel`: the cancelled lines are part of revising, and each is audited and emits `order.cancelled`. Lines keep their place (`line_number`, `lineNumber` in the answer), as they are written in one transaction at one time; the encounter's order list sorts by time and id, so a client sorts a prescription's lines by `lineNumber`. Any line can also be cancelled on its own as an order.
- **What a permission shows.** `encounter:read` gives the patient's name and MRN with each encounter, as `appointment:read` does with each appointment (its description says so), so a role given it reads who the visit is for. `order:read` includes medication orders with their dose and frequency, which the nurse needs to give them; `prescription:read` adds the prescription as a document (advice, revisions). Reading a prescription audits each of its lines as an order read too. `GET /encounters` counts `total` only with a `patientId` or `attendingDoctorId`: a whole hospital's encounters are not counted on every first page. It also takes a `type` (#83), for the patient's clinical timeline filtered by type of visit.
- **Every child names its encounter and patient together** (a foreign key to `encounters (tenant_id, id, patient_id)`), and amendments and replacements name one of the same encounter. Migration 0039 also gives `vitals.encounter_id` its reference, with the reading's patient. The item's name takes 340 characters: a drug's generic name, strength and brand at their longest.
- **Idempotency keys are required** on every POST, and nothing of an answer is kept (`body: 'none'`): creates set `request.idempotentId`, and a repeat answers with the record as it is now, audited as a read. Saving a draft (`PATCH`) and discarding one (`DELETE`) take no key: the version does that job, and a repeated discard is a 404.
- **Audit:** `encounter`, `clinical_note`, `diagnosis`, `order` and `prescription`, with the record's id: `create`, `update` (each change; a revision audits each line it cancels), `delete` (a discarded draft) and `read` for every row of a page. **Events** carry ids and codes only, never text: `encounter.opened` (patient, type, doctor, department, and the queue token and appointment when there are; triage attaches the token's readings), `encounter.closed` (with the appointment), `encounter.cancelled` (new), and `order.placed`, `order.cancelled`, `order.completed` (the order, encounter, patient, type, catalog id, prescription and priority). Accepted and in progress emit nothing yet.
- **Printing (#89)** is `POST /encounters/:encounterId/print` (the visit summary) and `POST /prescriptions/:prescriptionId/print`, for `encounter:print` with `encounter:read` for the summary and `prescription:read` for a prescription (admin, doctor and nurse by default), within the scope of each permission. A cancelled encounter, a replaced prescription and one with every line cancelled are refused (409 `not_printable`): they are not given to a patient. They write the audit entry `print` (entity `encounter` or `prescription`) and nothing else, answering 204: the web app makes the page from what it read. An idempotency key is required, one per press; a repeat records nothing more.
- **Credentials (#397):** each encounter answers with `attendingDoctorCredentials` and each prescription with `prescribedByCredentials` (`DoctorCredentials`: qualifications, registration number and council), read through `credentialsOf` from `features/doctors`, never its table, one query per answer or page. They are the profile as it is now, not copied onto the prescription: a registration number hardly changes, and a copy would add columns to an append-only table. Absent when the doctor has no profile at the hospital (an admin who prescribed), and a part the profile lacks is absent: the print says what is missing. Not PHI: they are printed for the patient.
- **Referrals and follow-ups (#88)** are this module's: tables `referrals` and `follow_ups` (migration 0041), each a child of the encounter (its encounter and patient together), with permissions `referral:read` and `referral:create` (doctor and admin; writing a referral needs both, as the answer holds the reason), `followUp:read` (doctor, nurse, receptionist and admin) and `followUp:create` (doctor and admin). Within the encounter's scope as every child (404 `referral_not_found`, `follow_up_not_found`).
- **A referral** names a department of the hospital not archived (422 `unknown_department`) and, or only, a practising doctor (422 `unknown_doctor`) of that department (422 `doctor_not_in_department`), with an urgency (`routine`, `urgent`) and a reason of plain lines; any doctor of the hospital, whatever the caller's scope, since a referral goes to someone else. Made only while the encounter is open; never changed, cancelled once with a reason (409 `already_cancelled`, the guard trigger and the column grants keep the rest), also after the encounter closed. Not an `Order`: nothing is billed or fulfilled by it.
- **A follow-up** is a calendar day at the hospital, today to a year ahead (422 `outside_window`), and a doctor (the encounter's when none is named; 422 `unknown_doctor`), with a note for the desk; one per encounter that is not cancelled (`follow_ups_one_per_encounter`, 409 `follow_up_exists`), set while the encounter is open. **Booking** (`POST /follow-ups/{id}/book`, `followUp:read` and `appointment:create`, and the appointments switch, 403 `feature_disabled`) books through `bookAppointment` from `features/appointments`, in the same transaction, with that route's rules, refusals, audit and `appointment.created`, and stores the appointment on the follow-up (`follow_ups_appointment` names the same patient, through `appointments_tenant_id_patient`, added by 0041). **Its state is read from that appointment** in SQL (`due`, `booked`, `attended`, `cancelled`), never stored, so a cancelled or missed appointment leaves it due again and nothing subscribes to appointment events; booking or cancelling needs it `due` (409 `invalid_transition`) at the version sent (409 `conflict`), its row locked. `GET /follow-ups` is the due list, soonest first, by state, days and doctor, within the encounter scope of `followUp:read`; it names the patient (name and MRN), as the permission says.
- **Scope and the due list.** A follow-up is seen within the encounter's scope of the route's permission: `followUp:read` to list and book (the desk's), `followUp:create` to cancel. `own` is the encounters the caller attends, as for every child of an encounter, not the follow-ups set to them by a colleague; a hospital that wants that needs a change here. The due list pages on `due_at`, a stored instant of the day (midnight UTC), so the partial index `follow_ups_tenant_due` orders and pages it; each state is a plain predicate (`IN_STATE`), never the CASE that answers it, and `total` comes only with a doctor or an encounter: a whole hospital is not counted on every first page. The guard trigger leaves `due_at` out, as a generated column is still null in a BEFORE trigger.
- **Rebooking replaces the link.** After the appointment is cancelled or missed, booking again stores the new appointment (`appointment_id`, `booked_at`, `booked_by`); the earlier one is in the audit log and its `followup.booked` event, not on the row. A table of every booking is the change if a hospital needs that history on the record.
- **The note is the desk's.** It is shown to whoever holds `followUp:read` (the receptionist by default), so the form says it is for what to bring or arrange, with no clinical detail. A field permission for it is the change if a hospital wants it narrower.
- **Cancelling an encounter** also refuses one with a referral or a follow-up that stands (`has_records`).
- **Events:** `referral.created` (the referral, encounter, patient, department, doctor, urgency), `referral.cancelled`, `followup.created` (with the doctor and day), `followup.booked` (with the appointment) and `followup.cancelled`: ids, codes and a day, never the reason or the note. The outbox takes no underscore before the dot, so `followup`, not `follow_up`. Audit entities `referral` and `follow_up`.
- **Lab orders belong to the laboratory while it is on** (#95): `POST /orders/:id/status` refuses a lab order when the hospital has the `laboratory` switch on (409 `lab_order`); the laboratory moves it through `labOrder` and `moveLabOrder` (`lab.ts`, exported from `index.ts`), which audit it and emit `order.completed` as the route does. Cancelling stays here.
- **Imaging orders belong to radiology while it is on** (#96), in the same way: 409 `imaging_order` with the `radiology` switch on; radiology moves them through `imagingOrder` and `moveImagingOrder` (`imaging.ts`). Both modules share `moveOrderOf` (`lab.ts`).
- **Not here:** charges for orders (billing, BE-13, reacts to `order.placed` and `order.completed`), samples and results (the lab module), completing the appointment when its encounter closes (an appointments subscriber of `encounter.closed`), and the web screens with their MSW mock.
- **Tests** clean up with `session_replication_role = replica` in one transaction, since the guard trigger refuses removing a signed note to everyone, the owner included (the test login is the database superuser, locally and in CI).
