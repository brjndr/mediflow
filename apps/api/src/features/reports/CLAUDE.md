# Reports API

What was decided for this feature's API (#123, BE-14). The root and `apps/api` `CLAUDE.md` rules apply. The web screen is `apps/web/src/features/reports` (#121).

## What exists

Behind the `reports` switch, with `report:read` and `report:export`, the admin's by default, both needing the scope `all`: reports are the whole hospital's money, stock and turnaround, so a custom role is given them on purpose.

- **Ten reports, one shape.** `opd_census`, `opd_hourly` (#725: OPD visits opened by day and hour 0 to 23 of the hospital's clock, only hours with a visit, counts only), `revenue`, `doctor_activity`, `lab_turnaround`, `pharmacy_sales`, `stock`, `collections`, `collections_by_close`, `outstanding` (`compute.ts`). Each answers a `ReportResult`: `columns` (a stable `key` and a `type`: `text`, `date`, `integer`, `money`, `minutes`), `rows` (arrays in column order), `totals` (a sum for a column that adds up, else null), the hospital's `currency`. Money is minor units; the web app translates the keys. A report holds counts, money and the names of catalog items (departments, tests, drugs), **never a patient's record**, which is why a stored result and a CSV may sit outside the audit trail of a patient read. Days are calendar days **at the hospital's clock** (`tenants.timezone`): a visit at 01:30 on the 10th is the 10th though it is the 9th in UTC. `stock` is a snapshot of now and takes no days.
- **What each counts.** Census: OPD encounters by the day opened, by department, with how many closed and cancelled. Revenue: invoices issued (`issued` and `paid`; a draft and a void invoice are not revenue) by issue day, credit notes by day, payments and refunds by day, and the net collected. Doctor-wise activity (#124): OPD encounters by attending doctor and the day opened (opened counts every visit opened, cancelled ones included; closed and cancelled are parts of it, so it is not "still open"), and that doctor's appointments in the range that were completed or no-shows; a doctor with neither is absent, and no patient is named. Pharmacy sales (#124): by day, counter sales (count and money), returns of counter sales, the net, and prescription dispenses (count, and units net of units brought back by a dispense return, on the day of the return; a dispense has no price here, billing charges it). Laboratory turnaround: results verified in the range (a correction is not a second result), minutes from the sample's collection to the verification, average, 90th percentile and maximum by test as it was entered. Collections (#691): payments and refunds by the day they were recorded, payment method (`cash`, `card`, `upi`, `bank`, `other`) and the staff member who recorded them (a name from `users`, never a patient); `netCollectedMinor` of a day equals collected less refunded of `revenue` for that day, and the cash line of a day is the cash that passed through the register sessions (opening excluded) of that day. Collections by close (#754, same columns): cash is dated by the day its register session closed, as the day-end settlement is, so a session across midnight is one day; money with no session (card, UPI, bank) keeps the day recorded, and cash of a session still open is left out until it closes. Outstanding (#691, a snapshot of now, no days): `issued` invoices with something due (`total - paid - credited`, paid being net of refunds), in three age bands since the issue day at the hospital's clock (`days_0_7`, `days_8_30`, `over_30`, always three rows) with the count and the amount due; no invoice number or patient, since the on-screen report names none. Stock: balances above zero by drug, batches, nearest expiry, units past their expiry and the value at cost (a batch with no cost is worth 0).
- **Answered on the spot** (`POST /reports/{kind}`, `report:read`): at most 31 days and 5,000 rows (422 `range_too_long`, `report_too_large`), under `REPORT_SYNC_TIMEOUT_MS` as a statement timeout the database enforces (422 `report_timeout`), and a hospital runs one at a time and the instance four (`ReportGate`, in-process like the rate limits: 429 `report_busy` with `Retry-After`). A POST so the filters stay out of logs. Audited as a read of `report`: the entry is written and the request's transaction ended **before** the report is worked out (`commitNow`), so the primary's connection is not held, idle, for as long as the report takes; the entry therefore records that a report was asked for, even if it then times out.
- **Stored** (`POST /reports/{kind}/runs`, `report:export`, `Idempotency-Key` required, answers 202): up to 366 days and 20,000 rows, made by the `reports.run` job and kept in `report_runs` with the result and a CSV in the file store. `GET /reports/runs` lists (no results), `GET /reports/runs/{id}` reads one with its result, `GET /reports/runs/{id}/download-url` gives the signed link. A repeat of one key answers the same run (`idempotent: { body: 'none' }`, found by `request.idempotentId`).
- **The CSV** has a byte order mark (Excel reads names in any script), the column keys as its header and a totals row. A text cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return gets a leading quote (`csv.ts`), so a department named to attack whoever opens the export does nothing. Numbers are never touched.
- **Downloading** (`GET /report-files/{runId}?expires&signature`): the signature (HMAC-SHA256 of the run id and the expiry with `REPORT_URL_SECRET`, `core/files/signing.ts`) is the only credential, because a link to object storage will have none either. The route is public (a per-address limit of 60 a minute, and the signature is checked before the database is reached), finds the run as the worker role (one policy and a grant of the columns it needs) and answers **one 404 `report_file_not_found` for everything wrong**: forged, altered, expired, a run that is gone, a file that is gone. The link lasts `REPORT_URL_TTL_SECONDS` (five minutes). **The audit entry is made when the link is issued** (`export` of `report_run`, by the person asking, in their hospital), not when it is used, since the use has no session. A download is logged with its run id (`report_file_downloaded`, ids only), so that it was taken and when can be told, not by whom. What is signed carries a purpose (`report-file.v1.`), so the key could sign another kind of link later without the two being interchangeable. A link lives out its few minutes after the person signs out or loses the permission. Give the link to the person who asked for it only.

## The replica and "a heavy report does not slow another hospital"

- **`app.database.reportPool`** is the read replica's own small pool when `DATABASE_REPLICA_URL` is set, else the primary's pool. Reports never use the request's transaction: `readAtHospital` opens a **read-only** transaction on that pool, `SET LOCAL ROLE mediflow_app`, sets `app.tenant_id` and `statement_timeout`, so row-level security applies on the replica exactly as on the primary. A replica may lag a little; a report can bear it. Do not use `reportPool` for anything that must see its own write.
- **What keeps one hospital from slowing the rest:** the time limit on every report (10 s on the spot, 120 s in a job), the per-hospital gate on the spot, the replica's own pool (a report cannot take a request's connection), and **the quota for stored reports**: `report_settings` (a row per hospital, optional) gives `max_concurrent` (2) waiting or running and `max_per_day` (50) in 24 hours, and `retention_days` (30); past either is 429 `report_quota` with `Retry-After` (60 s or an hour). A request takes an advisory lock on the hospital's quota before it counts, so two at once cannot both see room. The queue is pg-boss's, one job at a time a process, so a hospital with two in the queue holds two places and no more.
- **The app role only reads `report_settings`.** A hospital must not raise its own quota. The platform sets it (no screen or API yet; SQL for now) and `GET /reports/limits` shows the hospital what it has and has used.

## Jobs

- **`reports.run`** (`runReport`): claims the run (`queued`, or `running` and silent for longer than `REPORT_RUN_TIMEOUT_MS` plus five minutes, so a slow report is never taken from a live worker), works it out on the report pool, puts the CSV in the file store under `reports/<tenant>/<run>.csv` (so a second delivery writes the same file), then marks it `succeeded` with the result. A file written for a run that then fails, or that the sweep gave up meanwhile, is deleted at once. Any failure ends the run `failed` with a **code only** (`timeout`, `too_large`, `failed`), never the database's text, and is **not retried**: the person asks again. The job carries ids only; sent after the commit with the run's id as the job id.
- **`reports.sweep`** (hourly, as the worker role): gives up runs `queued` or `running` for an hour (`stalled`, so the quota is not held for ever; this also covers a process that stopped between the commit and the queueing) and deletes the runs past `expires_at` with their files, **files first and rows after**: the file's key is the run's own (`fileKeyOf`), whether or not the run named it, and a file that cannot be deleted keeps its run for the next sweep (logged as `report_file_delete_failed`, a count), so no file outlives its retention. 500 runs a sweep.

## Files (`core/files`)

`FileStore` (`put`, `get`, `delete`, optional `presign`) with `LocalFileStore`, a folder (`FILE_STORE_DIR`, `.data/files`, git-ignored). Keys are lower-case letters, digits and `/ _ . -` with no `..`, and a key that leaves the folder is refused. Good for development and a single container; **not for several API containers**, since each would have its own folder: the S3-compatible adapter (a bucket, server-side encryption, presigned URLs through `presign`) is the planned replacement and a configuration change for callers. Nothing is encrypted at rest by the local adapter.

## Known gaps

- Collections is by the day money was recorded; `collections_by_close` is the other grouping. Outstanding has no list of the invoices behind a band.
- No PDF or Excel export (CSV only; official documents are made on the server later), no scheduled or emailed reports.
- Revenue is by issue day, not accrual by service or department; credit notes are shown beside it, not netted from it. No report by payer or tax yet; doctor-wise activity counts encounters and appointments, not notes signed or prescriptions written.
- The gate and the sync limits are per API instance (N instances let one hospital run N at once); a Redis limiter replaces them with the rest.
- A run lost between the commit and the queueing waits up to an hour to be marked `stalled`.
- If the update that ends a run fails (the database is gone at that moment), the run stays `running`, holding quota, until the sweep: a retry of the job finds it not yet silent. Rare.
- The gate's limit of four reports for the instance is shared by all hospitals, so four hospitals each holding a report make a fifth get `report_busy` for a few seconds: fair to each hospital, a small coupling across them. A Postgres or Redis limiter replaces it.
- `report_runs.file_key` is a checked pattern, not tied to the run's own tenant and id by the database; only the job writes it.
- A failed run is not retried. A report that times out must be asked again over fewer days.
- The quota table has no API: the platform admin module will set it.
- Tests: `reports.test.ts`.
